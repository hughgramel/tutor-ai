# Problem & solution

## The problem (your words, p9)

> **People want to learn math in a more effective manner without paying for a tutor.**
> (+ outcomes, − price)

Unpacked:

- A human tutor works. It is also **$40–80/hr** and has to be scheduled.
- A textbook is cheap. It **doesn't respond** when you're stuck at step 2.
- ChatGPT is free and responds. It **gives you the answer**, so you learn nothing — this is your p3 note, *"AI is making us dummer."*
- Photomath/Gauth are instant. They are **answer machines**, not teachers.
- Math is **done by hand, on paper**. Every AI tool makes you leave the page to go type in a chat box. The medium of the help doesn't match the medium of the work.

**The gap:** there is no cheap thing that sits next to you *on the page*, watches you work by hand, and talks you through it — the way a tutor actually does.

## The persona (p8)

**John.** Learning algebra. Struggles with textbooks. Has used tutors before — they worked, but they're expensive. He wants to get better at math in a *guided* way, not be handed answers.

## The solution

An iPad + Apple Pencil canvas where you do your math by hand, and an AI tutor **sits on the page with you**:

- It **watches** your handwriting as you work.
- You **talk to it** — no typing, no leaving the page.
- It **writes back on the same page, by hand** — works an example, draws a diagram, circles the exact step you got wrong.
- It's **Socratic**: it makes you do the next one yourself.

One line: **an AI tutor that picks up the pencil next to you.**

## The bet

Every AI math product today either **solves for you** or **chats at you**.
Nobody has an AI that **writes on your paper while talking to you.**

Ink in, ink out, on the same page, out loud. That is the entire differentiator. If we
nail that one interaction, we have a product. If we don't, we have another chatbot.

## What we are NOT building (yet)

The notebook contains a **second, different company** (pp. 1–2 + the Philip Su meeting):
an **AI oral examiner** — pick what to assess, administer it via voice, grade it,
generate follow-ups, validate it against the SAT, sell it to the College Board.

That is B2B assessment. Different user, different buyer, different product. It is a
real idea with a real buyer, but it is **not the MVP**.

It comes back later as the *proof layer*: the assessment engine is how we demonstrate
that the tutor actually taught someone something (Philip's whole line of questioning).
Park it. Don't build it this weekend.

## Assumption → test → learn (p9)

- **Assume:** people will find an AI tutor effective for learning math, and it replicates
  the effectiveness of a human tutor.
- **Test:** put the prototype in front of (a) a real tutor — "would this help you / replace
  you?", or (b) a real student learning something new — teach them a lesson and *watch*.
- **Learn:** refine the prototype, or change the assumption.

The cheapest real test this week is **one student, one worksheet, one hour, watching.**
